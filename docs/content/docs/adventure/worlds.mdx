Put `data-world` on a screen, a section or a component, and everything inside picks up that world's colours.

```jsx
<section data-world="small_chops">…</section>
```

<WorldGrounds />

## What a world sets

- `--world-sky`, `--world-ground`, `--world-ground-deep` and `--world-bg` for the painted ground.
- `--world-accent` and `--world-accent-shade` for signs and chips.
- `--world-path` and `--world-path-edge` for the road.
- `--world-stroke` for outlined text.
- `--world-sky-low` for the horizon. A sky runs from `--world-sky` down to it.
- `--prop-hill`, `--prop-bush-hi`, `--prop-bush` and `--prop-bush-lo` for the CSS hills and bushes that stand in for painted art.

Without a `data-world`, you get the generic meadow.

## Rules

- Level numbers run on across worlds. They don't restart.
- Worlds can hold different numbers of levels. That's fine, and they're never padded out.
- The Summit is the author’s letter. Contributors can never choose it.
- Base Camp is where friends gather to pack their memories. It's the contributors' world, not a stop on the road, so it never shows on the map or in a world picker.

## Choosing a world

Contributors pick the world their memory belongs in.
